About Me

Sports AI engineer focused on computer vision for cricket and badminton. I build systems that survive messy, real-world video — not lab benchmarks.

I work on the full sports CV stack — from raw broadcast video to decisions a coach can act on. My day-to-day spans annotation infrastructure, ball and shuttle tracking, action recognition, pose-based biomechanics, and audio-visual event detection.

Outside of work, I run a long-running personal stack of cricket analytics projects — match predictors, venue intelligence, IPL auction strategy, fantasy AI. Most of them ship as live Streamlit or Tableau dashboards.

I think of myself as sports-first, algorithm-second. Every system I build starts with the sports question, then earns its architecture. Failure modes drive my design decisions, not benchmark numbers.

I'm based in India and open to roles in sports tech, franchise analytics, and broadcast AI.

How I Build
01
Sports-first framing
Every system starts with the sports question, not the algorithm. The right model for cricket is the wrong one for badminton.
02
Robustness over accuracy
Systems are built to survive occlusion, motion blur, and variable broadcast conditions — not to top a leaderboard.
03
Failure analysis as design
Breakdowns drive data and architecture decisions. Retraining is the last resort, not the first.
04
Decision-ready output
Every model answers one question: what does this enable a coach, analyst, or selector to do differently?
