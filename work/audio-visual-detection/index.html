Audio-Visual Bat-Ball Detection

An audio-first hit / miss classifier that works where vision alone fails — noisy training-net environments, low-light sessions, and feeds where the ball is occluded by the batter at contact.

What

Detects the bat-ball contact event from audio (and, where available, fuses it with vision). Outputs a frame-aligned timestamp and a hit / miss / edge classification.

Used to anchor downstream analysis — shot type starts at contact, ball speed is measured from contact, biomechanics segments split at contact.

Why

Bat-ball contact is the most informative moment in cricket video — but vision-based detection is unreliable. The batter's body occludes the ball; broadcast cameras don't always hold focus.

Audio gives a clean signal where vision is noisy. A multimodal system that leads with audio and supports with video works in training nets where pure-vision systems are useless.

How
  • Audio signal processing pipeline — spectral features tuned to the bat-ball impact signature.
  • Hit / miss / edge classifier trained on labelled training-net audio.
  • Vision fallback / fusion where the broadcast feed includes a clear contact frame.
  • Robust noise-floor calibration so the system works in echoey indoor nets as well as on open grounds.
Difficulties
  • Acoustic variability — wooden bat, training bat, throwdown sessions, leather vs tennis ball all produce different impact signatures.
  • Background noise — coaches shouting, crowd noise, other players hitting in adjacent nets.
  • Edge cases — a faint edge sounds nothing like a middle; the classifier had to handle a wide acoustic range without false-positiving on bat-pad contact.
  • Cross-modal alignment — getting audio timestamps frame-aligned with video for downstream consumers.
