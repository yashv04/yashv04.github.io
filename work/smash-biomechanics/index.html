Smash Technique & Biomechanics

A video-only biomechanics pipeline that derives joint kinematics and smash speed from shuttle trajectory and player keypoints. Zero wearables, zero markers.

What

Takes a smash sequence as input — outputs the player's joint angles, segment velocities, kinetic-chain timing, and the shuttle's smash speed derived from its trajectory.

Designed to give coaches a quantitative breakdown of technique without strapping sensors on athletes.

Why

Lab-based biomechanics requires markers, motion capture rigs, and controlled environments — none of which translate to a training session or a real match. Coaches don't get the data when they need it most.

A video-only pipeline puts biomechanics analysis into the gym, the academy court, and the broadcast feed — wherever the camera is, you get the numbers.

How
  • Mediapipe pose estimation for 2D keypoints; sport-specific joint definitions derived from those.
  • Shuttle trajectory (from the tracking system) converted into smash speed via frame-rate calibration and known court geometry.
  • Kinetic-chain timing extracted from joint-angle velocity profiles — shoulder-elbow-wrist sequencing.
  • Outputs visualized as overlaid kinematic plots a coach can read in seconds.
Difficulties
  • Single-camera depth ambiguity — 2D pose is fundamentally lossy; the pipeline had to be honest about what it could and couldn't infer.
  • Frame-rate sensitivity — at smash speeds, 30 fps video loses the peak; 60 fps was the practical minimum.
  • Court calibration — converting pixels to metres reliably across different camera placements without manual rig setup.
  • Trust — coaches won't use a tool they can't sanity-check; outputs had to be visually verifiable against the source clip.
